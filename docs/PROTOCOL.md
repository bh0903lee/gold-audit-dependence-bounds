# Protocol records of the prespecified studies

This document is an English transcription of the internal protocol records that were fixed before each study was run. For every study it gives the record date, the prespecified estimands, sample sizes, seeds and audit budgets, methods, decision rules and criteria, in the order of the article's sections. The records are transcribed, not revised: qualifications that a criterion is exploratory, or that a study was designed after earlier results were known, are kept as recorded, and criteria that later failed are listed as written. Rationales are given only where the record contains one. Configuration files named below are those of this package.

Record dates: main simulation study, 2026-10-06 (design and first criterion) and 2026-10-07 (three extension records); six-variable comparison and assumption-violation study, 2026-10-13; bounded false positives, 2026-10-14 (record and a pre-run amendment of the same day); general stationary laws, windows cut from one record, and the occupancy record, 2026-10-14 (design record, implementation contract, and, for the occupancy record, an amendment written after the data were obtained and before any interval was computed); paired comparison of the learned false-positive budget, 2026-10-15; SPHERE house recordings, 2026-10-22 (design record fixed before any cross-tabulation of the record was computed).

Notation follows the article: `s` is the stay probability of the latent chain, `m` the latent prevalence, `B` the audit budget, `K` the calibration budget, `N` the number of observation episodes, `alpha` the total error probability, `theta = E[(2 X_0 - 1)(2 X_1 - 1)]` the target, and `f = P(X_1 = 0, Y_1 = 1)` the false-positive mass. M, N and F denote marginal, neighbor and full audit information.

## 1. Main simulation study (article Sections 7.1-7.6)

Configuration: `configs/main.json` (seeds 91001-91200).

### 1.1 Design record (fixed by 2026-10-06)

- Question: with the same number of single-time gold audits, does using the neighboring noisy observations narrow the bound on the latent lag correlation relative to a marginal audit?
- Generator: stationary binary Markov latent process with stay probability 0.2, 0.5, 0.8; `Y_t = X_t D_t` with detection probability 0.95 or 0.20 according to the previous latent state. Independent episodes of length 3 are collected. In an audit episode only the central `X` is revealed; all three `Y` are observed.
- Linear program: the 27 word probabilities over the alphabet {(0,0), (1,0), (1,1)} are the variables; constraints are the observed word frequencies, stationarity, the audited `E[X_1]`, and optionally `E[X_1 Y_0]` and `E[X_1 Y_2]`.
- Baseline and fairness: marginal-audit outer program against neighbor-audit outer program, with the same number of audits; `E[X_0 X_1]` is never observed directly. A total `alpha = 0.05` is allocated by a union bound over all empirical constraints. Sample-wise nesting of the intervals is not guaranteed, because the allocation differs.
- Metrics: interval width for the true latent lag spin correlation, coverage, frequency of infeasible programs, audit budget. Failures are not deleted.
- Statistical unit and analysis: 200 independent seeds x 3 stay values x 5 budgets; paired width differences within a seed. The nominal 95% is the confidence-set guarantee of each configuration separately; no simultaneous 95% statement over all configurations is made. Coverage is reported with Monte Carlo standard errors and Wilson intervals.
- Go/no-go criterion: the primary budget is 500 gold audits. At this budget, the study evaluates whether the neighbor-audit outer width is reduced by at least 20% relative to the marginal audit in at least two of the three stay strata. Budgets 50, 100, 1000 and 5000 are sensitivity analyses; the most favorable budget is not selected to declare success. The record states that this is an exploratory definition fixed in response to a pre-run review of the plan, and that this is disclosed. Infeasible programs are reported as failure rows and frequencies, with coverage conditional on successful programs. The large conservatism of Hoeffding intervals is not evidence of sharp identification or of effective nominal coverage.
- Failure and analysis policy: completion conditions are journaled immediately; expected solver or inference failures are kept as status rows with the error; programming errors fail the run while preserving the partial journal; analyses report the failure rate and incompleteness together, and no automatic success is declared from successful rows alone.

### 1.2 Extension records (2026-10-07, written before each extension was run)

The record states that these extensions were designed after the results of the first run (Hoeffding intervals, marginal and neighbor audits) were known and are therefore reported as exploratory, and that seeds, budgets and criteria are not changed after the results are seen.

(a) First extension record. Changes: (1) empirical-Bernstein intervals (Maurer and Pontil, Theorem 4) with the same union allocation, in addition to Hoeffding; (2) a `full` audit moment set (the joint law of the audited `X_1` with the whole observed pattern `Y`, `2^L` constraints); (3) exact computation of the population identified set for each (stay, L, moment set), decomposing width into identified width plus concentration slack; (4) episode length `L = 4` as a sensitivity analysis. Primary criteria at budget 500, `L = 3`:

- C1: for the neighbor audit, the Bernstein interval reduces width relative to Hoeffding by at least 25% in at least two stay values, and Bernstein coverage is at least 0.95 in all conditions (200-seed Wilson lower limit reported).
- C2: under Bernstein intervals, the full audit reduces width relative to the neighbor audit by at least 10% in at least two stay values.
- Auxiliary: the ratio of concentration slack to width, convergence with the budget, and the replication of C2 at `L = 4`.
- If C1 and C2 both hold, the candidate claim is that the identification gain of neighbor and joint audit moments is realized in finite samples only with variance-adaptive intervals; if not, the result is reported as it is.

(b) Second extension record (same day, written before the run). Changes: (1) because all constraint indicators are Bernoulli, exact binomial (Clopper-Pearson) intervals with the same union allocation are added as a third interval method; (2) the finite-sample slack of the neighbor audit is decomposed into the share of the observation sample (50,000 episodes) and the share of the audit sample: the width with the observation moments fixed at their population values (audit slack), the width with the audit moments fixed (observation slack), and the width with both fixed (population identified width). Criteria at budget 500, `L = 3`, neighbor audit:

- E1: the exact binomial interval reduces width relative to Bernstein by at least 10% in at least two stay values, with coverage at least 0.95 maintained.
- D1: the audit slack ratio `(W_exact-obs - W_pop) / (W_both - W_pop)` is reported for each stay value; a ratio of at least 0.5 is named "the audit sample is the limit", otherwise "the observation sample is the limit". The record gives the rationale that this decomposition is the basis for the direction of a budget increase (more audits or more observations).

(c) Third record (same day; implementation corrections, criteria unchanged). (1) The empirical-Bernstein radius was corrected to the two-sided form, `ln(2/delta)` to `ln(4/delta)`, because Theorem 4 of Maurer and Pontil is one-sided; the earlier Bernstein results had at most a 90% rather than 95% guarantee. (2) When the confidence set is empty and the program fails, the row is kept with the flag `empty_confidence_set = True` and the conservative interval [-1, 1]; coverage counting empty sets as misses is reported alongside. (3) The exact binomial coverage test was corrected to use the two endpoints of the same sample. E1 and D1 are recomputed with the corrected implementation; the interpretation remains exploratory.

## 2. Six-variable comparison (article Section 7.6)

Record of 2026-10-13, written before any numerical result of the six-variable program, the comparators or the violation simulators had been computed; the analysis scripts were written before the runs and tested on smoke output only. Configuration: `configs/main_w3.json`.

Data. `L = 3`, `N = 50,000`, `B` in {50, 100, 500, 1000, 5000}, `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}, detector (0.95, 0.20), `alpha = 0.05`.
(A) The archived arrays of the main study (seeds 91001-91200); because the word-program rows had already been published, their reuse is called a reanalysis only.
(B) New seeds 92001-92200 (confirmation run; the P1 and P2 decisions use this set).
(C) Extension seeds 92201-93000 with the same configuration, combined with (B) for a 1000-seed coverage estimate. (A) and (B) are not mixed and are tabulated separately. Random-number consumption for new seeds: `default_rng(seed)`, then stay order, then the observation cohort, then the audit cohorts in budget order.

Methods (all on the same samples, total `alpha = 0.05`, the same information: observed `Y` triples and the audited `X_1, Y_0, Y_2`).

- M1: neighbor word program, Hoeffding / empirical-Bernstein / Clopper-Pearson, `M = 11`, `delta = alpha/11`.
- M2: six-variable program, Clopper-Pearson, five intervals each at `delta = alpha/5`; `m, a, b` from the audit sample (`X_1`, `Y_0 X_1`, `X_1 Y_2`); `y, h` from the observation cohort (`Y_1`, `Y_0 Y_1`; `Y_1 Y_2` is not used). Variables `(m, y, a, b, h, t)`, nonnegativity of the nine entries of `K(t)`, minimum and maximum of `1 - 4m + 4a + 4b - 4h + 4t`. If infeasible, fallback [-1, 1] with a flag.
- M3: six-variable program with Hoeffding and two-sided empirical-Bernstein (`ln(4/delta)`) intervals, `delta = alpha/5`.
- M4: plug-in of the closed-form endpoints with a percentile bootstrap: 2,000 multinomial resamples of the observation cohort (8 patterns) and of the audit cohort (5 cells); interval `[q_{alpha/2}(theta_L*), q_{1-alpha/2}(theta_U*)]` truncated to [-1, 1]; if the lower limit exceeds the upper, an empty interval (miss, width 0, flagged). No finite-sample guarantee.
- M5: Dirichlet(1/2) prior on the observation 8-cell and audit 5-cell multinomials; 4,000 posterior draws; closed-form endpoints; `[q_{alpha/2}(theta_L), q_{1-alpha/2}(theta_U)]`. Posterior draws incompatible by the compatibility corollary are discarded and their fraction reported; if fewer than 100 compatible draws remain, fallback [-1, 1] with a flag. No frequentist guarantee.
- M6: two L1 balls (Weissman et al. 2003) for the observation 8-cell and the audit 5-cell distributions, each at `delta' = alpha/2` with radius `sqrt(2 ln((2^k - 2)/delta') / n)`, linearized with auxiliary variables and projected through the word program. Guaranteed (union of two regions).
- The internal random numbers of M4 and M5 use `default_rng([seed, round(1000 s), B, method number])`, separate from the sampling stream.

Metrics. Mean of the paired relative width reduction `100 (1 - W_cand / W_ref)` within seeds with a Student t 95% Monte Carlo interval; coverage of the true theta (Wilson 95%); fraction of samples containing the whole population identified interval (tolerance 1e-9); fallback and empty-interval fractions with denominators preserved.

Criteria.

- P1 (primary; `B = 500`, reference M1 with Clopper-Pearson, candidate M2): mean paired reduction of at least 3 percentage points in all three stay values with the 95% Monte Carlo lower limit above 0; M2 coverage at least 0.95; M2 containment of the whole population identified interval at least 0.95. If met, the wording is "uniformly, modestly shorter".
- P2 (secondary): mean reduction of at least 10 percentage points in at least two stay values. P1 met and P2 not met gives the wording "modest".
- Negative result: if in any stay value the mean reduction is below 0 with the Monte Carlo upper limit below 0 (M2 wider), or the Monte Carlo interval contains 0, that regime is reported as "finite-sample neutral or harmful". The coverage statement of the corollary is unaffected.
- External comparison: M4 and M5 are compared on width only in cells with coverage at least 0.95 and Wilson lower limit at least 0.93 (1000 seeds; `B` in {100, 500} are the primary cells); cells below this are marked "shorter but invalid". M6 is compared with M1 (Clopper-Pearson) on width; the prior prediction is that it is wider.
- Prior predictions: a net gain of 5-10% for M2; M4 the shortest but with possible coverage below 0.95 at `s = 0.5` or `B <= 100`.
- Multiplicity: P1 and P2 are the only primary family; stay values are handled by the "all" / "at least two" rules. Other budgets, M3-M6 and the 1000-seed width comparisons are exploratory. The narrowest method is not selected after the fact.

Precision statement recorded in the plan from which this record was made (2026-10-13): the 95% Monte Carlo half-widths of the 200-seed paired comparisons of the main study were 1-2 percentage points, so 200 seeds detect a 5-point difference, with a minimum detectable difference of about 2-3 points.

## 3. Assumption-violation study (article Section 7.7)

Record of 2026-10-13 (same record as Section 2), with a pre-run correction of the same day made when no result existed for any registered seed. Configurations: `configs/main_w2a.json` (1000 seeds), `configs/main_w2b.json` (200 seeds).

Common. `L = 3`, `N = 50,000`, `alpha = 0.05`, `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}; the baseline generator is that of the main study. One axis is changed at a time. Methods: neighbor word program with Clopper-Pearson intervals (the primary method), the six-variable program with Clopper-Pearson intervals, and, for reference, the neighbor word program with Hoeffding intervals. The target theta is the true latent lag-one spin moment of the observation-cohort population (also under audit-cohort drift). Common random numbers `default_rng([seed, stay index, 2])` are shared across levels, so cells of different levels within a seed are not independent. At level 0 the simulators of axes V1, V2a, V2b, V4a, V4b and V5 must reproduce the baseline random stream and arrays (unit test). Axes V3a, V3b and V6 have no level 0 or consume random numbers differently and are not analyzed as paired with the baseline.

| Axis | Violation | Levels (excluding 0) | Violates the theorem's assumptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | `Y_t = X_t D_t + (1 - X_t) F_t`, `P(F = 1) = phi` (both cohorts) | phi in {0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10} | yes |
| V2a | audit cohort only: stay `s + Delta`, or detector `(0.95 - Delta_d, 0.20 + Delta_d)` | Delta in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20} (Delta_d = 0); Delta_d in {0.05, 0.10} (Delta = 0) | yes |
| V2b | at positions t = 0, 1, 2, `P(D_t = 1 | X_{t-1} = 0) = 0.95 - kappa t` (both cohorts) | kappa in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20} | yes |
| V3a | symmetric second-order Markov latent chain: stay `s + g(1 - s)` after a stay, `s - g s` after a transition | g in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75} | no (generality check) |
| V3b | detector depending on `X_{t-2}` (`P(D = 1 | X_{t-1}, X_{t-2}) = [[0.95, 0.70], [0.35, 0.05]]`) or on `D_{t-1}` (`P(D_t = 1 | X_{t-1}, D_{t-1}) = [[0.80, 0.99], [0.05, 0.50]]`) | 2 variants | no (generality check) |
| V4a | the revealed `X_1` is flipped with probability epsilon | epsilon in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05} | yes |
| V4b | recorded as 0 with probability epsilon only when `X_1 = 1, Y_1 = 0` (anchoring) | epsilon in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20} | yes |
| V5 | `P(audit | Y_1 = 1) / P(audit | Y_1 = 0) = rho` (rejection sampling to exactly B audits) | rho in {2, 4} | yes |
| V6 | N length-3 windows at spacing `step` from one long sequence (observation); B windows from an independent long sequence (audit) | step in {1, 3, 30} | yes (independent episodes) |

The true value under V3a is `theta = 2q - 1`, `q = p_cs / (1 - p_ss + p_cs)`. Reductions relative to the plan, stated before the run: the V2b variant with a position-dependent latent marginal is excluded because theta is then not a single number; the periodic-mixture variant of V3a is omitted because it is covered by the theorem verifier, leaving the three second-order chains; the optional four-state robust program is not run in this study.

Seeds and cells. 1000 seeds (92001-93000): axes V1, V2a, V4a, V4b, V5 and the baseline; `B` in {500, 5000}; word program and six-variable program, both Clopper-Pearson. 200 seeds (93001-93200): all axes and the baseline; `B` in {100, 500, 5000}; three methods. The two sets are not mixed; the primary cells are decided on the 1000-seed set, and V2b, V3, V6, `B = 100` and Hoeffding on the 200-seed set. Seed numbers shared with the six-variable comparison are not repetitions of the same experiment because streams and conditions differ.

Estimands. (i) The contaminated population moments `(m*, y*, a*, b*, h*)` by exact enumeration, the closed-form interval `[theta_L*, theta_U*]`, and `d = max(theta_L* - theta, theta - theta_U*, 0)` (deterministic), together with the compatibility of the contaminated moments and the feasibility and deviation of the word program's population interval at those moments; (ii) finite-sample coverage of the true theta, with the fallback [-1, 1] counted as covering (primary) and empty sets counted as misses (alongside); (iii) width; (iv) fraction of empty confidence sets; (v) T1: the one-sided model is rejected when the audit sample contains at least one record with `X_1 = 0, Y_1 = 1`; rejection rate (compared with the analytic `1 - (1 - p)^B`) and coverage conditional on passing T1. Breakdown boundaries are also reported on a fine grid of `d`, independent of the levels.

Criteria (decision cell: word program with Clopper-Pearson intervals, `B = 500`; six-variable program alongside; coverage decisions on the point estimate with Wilson 95% intervals alongside).

- R1 (V3): in all V3 conditions `d = 0` (tolerance 1e-9) and the Wilson lower limit of coverage is at least 0.92. A failure is treated as a signal of a bug or a theoretical error and investigated before reporting.
- R2 (V1): report `phi*(s) = inf{phi : d > 0}`; the largest level phi at which coverage at `B = 500` stays at least 0.90; the largest level at which coverage conditional on passing T1 stays at least 0.95. Prior prediction: `d` is positive as soon as `phi > 0`, and the slack at `B = 500` masks this up to about `phi = 0.01`.
- R3 (V2a): if coverage is at least 0.90 in all three stay values at `Delta = 0.05`, "small drift tolerated"; if below 0.90 in any stay value at `Delta = 0.10`, "cohort simultaneity required". `Delta_d` is described the same way.
- R4 (V4): whether coverage is at least 0.90 at `epsilon = 0.01` under V4a; whether `d` under V4b exceeds `d` under V4a at the common level `epsilon = 0.05` (prior prediction: anchoring is more harmful).
- R5 (V5): whether `d > 0` at `rho = 2` (prior prediction: yes).
- R6 (V6): whether any budget has coverage below 0.95 at step 1.
- Negative result: if any stay value already has coverage below 0.90 at `B = 500` for `phi = 0.002`, the conclusion is "extremely sensitive to one-sided error". A collapse at the lowest level of any axis is reported as such.
- Multiplicity: Wilson intervals per cell only; no simultaneous guarantee over the family. Width comparisons between the six-variable and word programs under violation are exploratory.

Verification (not a result). Unit-test seeds do not overlap the study seeds 91001-93200. A 2,000-seed check of the six-variable program's coverage without violation (`s = 0.5`, `B = 500`, seeds 70001-72000) is an implementation check, not evidence for the decisions.

Pre-run correction (2026-10-13). (1) The random-number key of this study was changed from `[seed, stay index]` to `[seed, stay index, 2]`, because `[seed, 0]` would have produced the same stream as `default_rng(seed)` used in the six-variable comparison. (2) The axes to which the level-0 stream requirement applies were specified as above. (3) The six-variable program is re-solved once without presolve when residuals exceed tolerance before being declared failed (numerical stabilization; the interval definition is unchanged). Criteria, levels, seeds and methods were not changed.

## 4. Bounded false positives (article Section 8.1)

Record of 2026-10-14 (design fixed after an independent design review; no new experimental code or output had been produced or viewed), with a pre-run amendment of the same day. Configuration: `extensions/configs/protocol.json`.

Hypotheses and claims. Target `theta` as in the article, distinguished from the Pearson correlation when `m` differs from 0.5. Tolerance `f = P(X_1 = 0, Y_1 = 1) <= epsilon`; `f` is not identified with the generator's conditional rate `phi`. Assumptions: joint stationarity, error-free central gold labels, representative episode samples; the frequentist guarantee is restricted to independent episodes within a cohort; allowing false positives does not repair drift, selection, gold errors or episode dependence.

- T1 (proof obligation): sharpness of the four-state word program, recovery of the three-state program at `epsilon = 0`, nesting in `epsilon`; stated as an application of existing linear-programming principles, not as a new principle.
- T2 (provisional theoretical claim): for a compatible full three-point law `H`, the 16-variable pair program on the eight local moments and the full 64-variable word program give the same `theta` interval and admit a stationary extension preserving `H`; an extension of the three-state gluing; no minimality claim.
- H1 (open existence hypothesis): with `f > 0`, the information set N6 (`m, a, b, j`), which adds the central moment `j = E[X_1 Y_1]`, can be strictly wider than full information F. A gap between N (`m, a, b`) and F alone does not support H1.
- H2 (finite sample): the four-state neighbor program with the one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper limit from an independent representative calibration audit has coverage at least 0.95 for the true population identified set; the proof and the Monte Carlo check are separate; no width improvement is promised.
- H3 (practicality, secondary): with a large calibration budget, `U - f` and the mean width excess over the oracle may decrease; monotonicity is not required; a non-decrease is reported as such.

Linear program. Alphabet `Z = 0:(0,0), 1:(1,0), 2:(1,1), 3:(0,1)` with explicit lookups for `X` and `Y`; `L = 3` primary, `L = 4` secondary; nonnegativity, normalization, prefix/suffix word-mass equality; `f(p) <= epsilon`; the target is the mean adjacent spin product, which under stationarity equals the first-pair target. Each information set receives only the observed `Y` law and audit moments it permits. Information hierarchy: N (`m, a, b`), N6 (`m, a, b, j`), N7L (N6 plus `c_L`), N7R (N6 plus `c_R`), N8 (N6 plus `c_L, c_R`), F (full `H(Y_0, X_1, Y_1, Y_2)`), where `j = E[X_1 Y_1]`, `c_L = E[Y_0 X_1 Y_1]`, `c_R = E[X_1 Y_1 Y_2]`; M8 is the pair formulation on the eight moments `(m, y, a, b, h, j, c_L, c_R)` without the observed triple law. Gluing certificate obligations: the pair matrix `K >= 0` with equal row and column marginals, the coarsening identities, the triple construction `T[i,z,k] = H[g(i),z,g(k)] K[i,z]/H_L[g(i),z] K[z,k]/H_R[z,g(k)]` with zero terms where denominators vanish, and verification of nonnegativity, both pair marginals, recovery of `H`, agreement of the objectives, the pair-state chain and attainability of the whole interval; exact rational certificates with stored `K, T, H`; at least one law with `f > 0` with lower and upper certificates checked by an independently implemented checker.

Population study. Prespecified law banks: (A) recovery controls: the baseline Markov detector laws with `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}, detectors (0.95, 0.20) and (0.80, 0.80), `phi = 0`, at `L = 3` and `L = 4` (closed-form equivalence required at `L = 3` only), plus 128 ternary periodic mixtures (seeds 300001-300128); (B) two-sided parametric grid: `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}, the same two detectors, `phi` in {0.002, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10}, 24 laws; (C) general stationary rational bank: 512 laws (seeds 310001-310512), each a mixture of three cyclic words with lengths drawn uniformly from {3, ..., 8}, symbols drawn uniformly from the four states, integer mixture weights from {1, ..., 5} normalized, uniform phase; the scheme is fixed before the run and no further laws are generated in search of a gap; laws with `f = 0` or degenerate laws are tagged, not excluded. For each law with positive `f`, `epsilon` in {`f`, min(1, `f` + 0.005), min(1, `f` + 0.02), min(1, `f` + 0.10)}, duplicates computed once; the same increments for `f = 0`. All of N, N6, N7L, N7R, N8, F and the M8 pair program are computed on each law; the `L = 4` secondary comparison uses the first 64 laws of bank C, with F holding the audit cross masses of `X_1` with all 16 `Y_0:Y_3` patterns, and equivalence with F is not required at `L = 4`. Metrics: endpoints, width, `gap_lower = L_F - L_N6`, `gap_upper = U_N6 - U_F`, `gap_max`; mechanism tables for N and N7/N8 against F; maximum endpoint error of N8 against F, closed-form error at `epsilon = 0`, maximum nesting violation. A gap is declared in the search when the floating-point gap exceeds 1e-6; the final evidence of existence must be a strict gap in an exact rational optimality certificate; the law is selected as the smallest seed with a gap above 1e-6 and, within a seed, the smallest `epsilon`; a large counterexample is not chosen as the representative. Tolerances: numerical equivalence 1e-8; primal and dual residuals 1e-9 targeted, above 1e-7 solver-invalid; one retry without presolve; persistent failures are stored with their status, excluded from the evidence for the claim and kept in the denominator. Success and refutation: T1 and T2 verification, recovery at `epsilon = 0` and a certificate for a law with `f > 0` are necessary; a proven strict gap between N6 and F supports H1; a gap for N alone is explained by the missing central moment and does not support H1; no gap in the 512 laws is not a proof of equivalence; the M8 equivalence is not claimed as a new program or as minimality.

Finite-sample study. `L = 3`, `N = 10,000` independent episodes; total gold budget `T` in {200, 1000, 4000}, split into `B = T/2` moment-audit episodes and `K = T/2` calibration episodes generated independently from the same population; only the central `X_1` is provided to the inference. Primary: `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8} x `phi` in {0, 0.02, 0.10}, detector (0.95, 0.20), `T = 1000`, nine cells, seeds 410001-411000 (1000 replicates per cell). Secondary: the same nine cells at `T = 200` and `T = 4000`, seeds 420001-420200. Prevalence check (separate table, not in the primary family): `m` in {0.2, 0.8}, lag persistence `rho = 0.6` with `P01 = m(1 - rho)`, `P10 = (1 - m)(1 - rho)`, detector (0.95, 0.20), `phi = 0.10`, `T = 1000`, seeds 430001-430200; the true joint `f = (1 - m) phi` and `theta` are computed by independent enumeration. Random numbers: `SeedSequence([20261014, 11, 2, condition_id, seed, cohort_id])`, cohorts 0 (observation), 1 (moment audit), 2 (calibration), 9 (analysis bootstrap). Methods on the same samples: `naive_zero` (three-state neighbor program, `epsilon = 0`, Clopper-Pearson `alpha = 0.05`; no guarantee under violation); `oracle_joint` (four-state neighbor program, `epsilon` = true `f`, moment error 0.05; an infeasible control); `learned_upper` (primary: four-state neighbor program, `epsilon = U_CP(k, K; 0.99)`, moment error 0.04 split over the 8 patterns and 3 audit moments, calibration error 0.01; `U > 0` when `k = 0`, `U = 1` when `k = K`); `plugin_joint` (`epsilon = k/K`, moment error 0.05; a negative control that ignores the estimation error of `epsilon`). Metrics per replicate: `contains_population` (the sampled outer interval contains the whole identified interval of the true population under N information at `epsilon` = true `f`, tolerance 1e-8), `covers_truth`, width (domain [-1, 1]), empty set, solver failure, fallback used, nonempty coverage, whether `epsilon` covers `f`, `U - f`, width minus oracle width. Empty confidence sets and solver failures are never merged with successes; raw empty-set coverage is 0 and fallback-inclusive coverage is reported alongside; solver failures are marked unknown and counted as misses in the conservative summary. Confidence intervals and rules: for `contains_population` of `learned_upper` in the nine primary cells, two-sided Clopper-Pearson Monte Carlo intervals at level `1 - 0.05/9`; truth coverage per cell with 95% intervals; theoretical success is a proof of 0.95 under the stated assumptions passing an independent checker; the implementation check that excludes a large coverage loss requires all nine simultaneous lower limits at least 0.93 and is not called a proof of 0.95 coverage; any upper limit below 0.95 is a refutation signal; anything else is insufficient Monte Carlo evidence; seed counts are not increased after results are reported. Width excess relative to the oracle: seed-paired means with 10,000 paired-bootstrap 95% intervals (secondary); a width-superiority claim in the primary cells would require Bonferroni `1 - 0.05/9` intervals and is not a condition of acceptance. Practicality criterion fixed in advance: in at least three of the six primary cells with nonzero false positives, fallback-inclusive mean width below 1.8 and fallback rate below 0.05, in which case only "nontrivial precision in the tested cells" is reported; otherwise the conclusion is that the method is practically almost vacuous there. Decision priority: (1) any primary cell whose simultaneous Monte Carlo upper limit is below 0.95 is a refutation signal; (2) otherwise, all nine lower limits at least 0.93 passes the implementation check; (3) otherwise insufficient. Nesting diagnostic (separate): for the first 50 seeds of each primary cell, one master Clopper-Pearson box over the 8 observation and 16 audit cells (error 0.04/24) with the same calibration limit; F uses the master constraints and N, N6, N8 receive only outer aggregations of the same cell limits, so that F is nested in N8, N6 and N; this is an implementation check of information nesting, not a statement about the width order of the practical procedures. Resource limits were fixed in advance (CPU only, at most 8 workers, one thread per worker, 8 CPU-hours, 2 wall-hours, 8 GB memory, 2 GB output); partial runs are not reported as complete and favorable cells are not selected.

Pre-run amendment (2026-10-14, before the full population and finite-sample runs). (1) Infeasibility is accepted only after a retry without presolve; empty intervals carry no endpoints. (2) Solver failures receive the reporting fallback [-1, 1] with width 2 and are counted as fallback used, while remaining coverage misses. (3) Nesting pairs that include a solver failure are recorded as unchecked and counted. (4) Additional counters for pair-program failures and for equivalence and recovery checks that could not be made. Disclosure recorded in the amendment: before the runs, a code reviewer had computed the population endpoints of bank C seeds 310001-310040 for cross-validation and had seen N6-F gaps up to 0.77; these deterministic values did not use the confirmation seeds. Not changed: only primal residuals are checked; several auxiliary quantities are examined after the run rather than recorded during it.

Question. The finite-sample evidence of the main study (neighbor audit 6.2-34.3% narrower than the marginal audit) came from the symmetric Markov detector family only; does the finite-sample gain of the neighbor audit appear for the general stationary laws that the theorem addresses?

Laws (fixed rule from the existing banks): without false positives, the 40 ternary periodic mixtures of bank A with seeds 300001-300040; with false positives, bank C seeds 310041-310080 (40 laws), excluding seeds 310001-310040 whose population endpoints had been computed by a reviewer (Section 4); bank C laws are analyzed with the learned upper limit. Degenerate laws (zero population width, or `f = 0` in bank C) are tagged and not excluded.

Samples. Independent episodes, `N = 10,000`; `B` in {200, 500, 2000}; `K = B` for laws with false positives; 200 replicates per law and budget, seeds 450001-450200; random numbers `SeedSequence([20261014, 11, 4, condition_id, seed, cohort_id])` with `condition_id = 3 * law_index + budget_index`, cohorts 0 (observation), 1 (audit), 2 (calibration), 9 (analysis); laws are converted exactly to probability vectors and words are sampled independently; M, N and F share the samples and `epsilon`. Implementation contract: direct Clopper-Pearson intervals with the moment error split equally over the 9, 11 and 24 constraints of M, N and F; bank A with `epsilon = 0` and moment error 0.05; bank C always with the learned upper limit (`K = B`, moment error 0.04, calibration error 0.01) even when the true `f` is 0. `contains_population` for each method is containment of that method's own population identified interval (computed with the method's information and the true `f`, tolerance 1e-8); the common point-target coverage is reported separately. Solver failures are misses, empty sets raw misses, both with reported width 2 and fallback flag; undefined population references are recorded as undefined with both denominators. Relative width reduction is the ratio of mean widths, `1 - mean(W_strong) / mean(W_weak)`, with fallback widths 2 included; a zero denominator is reported with its reason and the law is not deleted; strictness tolerance 1e-8.

Hypotheses (descriptive emphasis).

- H9a (implementation check): for every law and budget, the Monte Carlo 95% Clopper-Pearson lower limit of N's `contains_population` is at least 0.93; laws that violate this are listed as they are; no simultaneous guarantee over the 240 law-budget cells is claimed.

Laws with an undefined ratio or a failed reference stay in the list and are not counted as successes; the number of comparable laws is reported; an incomplete grid blocks the decision. The median and the 10th and 90th percentiles of the relative reduction (linear quantile method) are reported for all laws and by bank, and the position of the main study's Markov regimes in this distribution is indicated.
- Refutation interpretation: below the threshold, the article's finite-sample statement is restricted to the Markov detector family.

## 6. Paired comparison of the learned and the true false-positive budget (article Section 8.2)

Record of 2026-10-15 (design fixed after the results of Sections 4, 5, 7 and 8 were known; seeds and decision rules frozen before any new result was opened; the design states that it is a follow-up of known results). Configuration: `paired_budget/configs/p2.json`.

Laws: the 40 bank C laws (seeds 310041-310080); the true `f*` is 0 for two of them. Arms: `oracle_f_05` (`epsilon = f*`, moment error 0.05), `oracle_f_04` (`epsilon = f*`, moment error 0.04), `learned` (`epsilon` = calibration Clopper-Pearson upper limit, moment error 0.04 plus calibration error 0.01), `plugin` (`epsilon = k/K`, moment error 0.05; a diagnostic without guarantee). `known_zero` (`epsilon = 0`, moment error 0.05) is run only on the two laws with `f* = 0` as an appendix diagnostic. The oracle constraint is the upper bound `f <= epsilon`. Reference: for each law and information set, the identified interval at the true `f*` and the true moments, and the true `theta`; population intervals built from a sample's `epsilon` are auxiliary width-decomposition quantities with a separate name. Samples: `N = 10,000`, `B` in {200, 500, 2000}, `K = B`, 200 replicates, seeds 480001-480200; the four arms share the observation, audit and calibration samples (`SeedSequence([20261014, 11, 8, condition_id, seed, cohort])`, `condition_id = 3 * zero_based_law_index + budget_index`). Statistic: for law l, arm a and budget B, `g_{l,a} = 1 - mean(W_N) / mean(W_M)` over the 200 replicates with fallback width 2 included (the definition of Section 5); effect `Delta_l = g_{l,oracle_f_05} - g_{l,learned}` (paired within law); primary quantity: `B = 500`, the median of `Delta_l` over the fixed panel of 40 laws. Monte Carlo uncertainty: 2,000 paired percentile bootstrap resamples of the within-law replicate index, applied jointly to the four arms and to M, N and F (the 95% interval of the median); resampling of laws is a separate sensitivity analysis; `np.quantile` with the linear method; the intervals are approximate Monte Carlo uncertainty, not exact coverage.

Hypotheses (one-sided; family error 0.05 split over two, each 0.025; later fixed to a single confirmatory hypothesis with `alpha = 0.025` and no redistribution):

- H1 (confirmatory): median `Delta_l >= 0.05` and the 97.5% Monte Carlo lower limit above 0 (the cost of the whole budget-learning procedure, including the change of the moment error).
- H1' (descriptive): `oracle_f_05 - oracle_f_04` and `oracle_f_04 - learned` reported separately, without a prediction of which is larger.
- H2 (descriptive): whether, after the `oracle_f_05` treatment, the bank C median remains below the stored bank A value (23.07%); a residual descriptive comparison, not a decomposition of causes.

Reporting and rules: coverage against the fixed reference and width at all budgets; denominators for empty sets, fallbacks and failures kept fixed; fallback width 2 applied to all arms; if `mean(W_M) = 0` the ratio is not formed and the reason is recorded, the median is computed over defined laws with their number reported, and the 40-law confirmatory decision is `not_evaluable`; the sum of medians of the two components is not presented as a decomposition; implementation check that `known_zero` and `oracle_f_05` coincide on the two laws with `f* = 0`; the full grid of 291,600 sample intervals must be complete before a decision.

## 7. Windows cut from one record (article Section 8.3)

Design record and implementation contract of 2026-10-14. Configuration: `extensions/configs/addendum_e7_e9.json`, key `e8`.

Question. How much does coverage break when the Clopper-Pearson procedure built for independent episodes is applied to all (overlapping) three-point windows of one record, and do two simple treatments (thinning, block means) restore it, at what cost in width?

Generator. Only mixing stationary processes; periodic mixtures are stationary but not ergodic, so one record does not reveal the population law, and they are excluded with this reason recorded. Latent symmetric two-state Markov chain with `s` in {0.2, 0.5, 0.8}, detector (0.95, 0.20), `phi` in {0, 0.02}: six primary cells (`condition_id` 0-5 ordered by `s` then `phi`); one auxiliary descriptive cell with an asymmetric chain, `m = 0.2`, `rho = 0.6`, `phi = 0.02`. `P(Y_t = 1 | X_t = 1, X_{t-1}) = detect[X_{t-1}]`, `phi` when `X_t = 0`; the chain starts from `X[-1] ~ Bernoulli(m)`.

Samples. Record length 10,002, hence 10,000 consecutive windows (the observation count of the independent baseline); `B = 500` audit centers by simple random sampling without replacement; for `phi > 0`, `K = 500` calibration windows from a separate independent record of the same length; the independent baseline generates 10,000 episodes, 500 audits and 500 calibration episodes separately. Random numbers `SeedSequence([20261014, 11, 3, condition_id, seed, cohort_id])`, cohorts 0 (record), 1 (audit priorities), 2 (calibration record), 3 (calibration priorities), 4-6 (independent baseline), 9 (analysis bootstrap). Each center receives one uniform priority, and the `B = 500` audits are the smallest priorities within the eligible set of each method, so the methods are coupled.

Methods. (1) independent-episode baseline; (2) all overlapping windows treated as independent (naive); (3) thinning to every third center with a fresh selection of 500 audits from the thinned set, calibration thinned the same way; (4) thinning to every tenth center, likewise; (5) and (6) block means over consecutive blocks of 50 or 200 centers: observation moments are the equal-weight means of the block means of each Bernoulli feature; the audits are the same 500 as the naive method, attributed to their blocks, with equal weights over nonempty blocks (empty blocks excluded, not set to 0; block counts recorded). Block confidence intervals: for J block means in [0, 1], center `mean(z_j)`, two-sided radius `sqrt(log(2/delta) / (2J))` intersected with [0, 1], `delta` = moment error / 11, J = number of blocks (observation) or nonempty blocks (audit). Calibration for blocks: one-sided radius `sqrt(log(1/0.01) / (2 J_cal))`, upper limit `min(1, center + radius)`; other methods use the Clopper-Pearson upper limit. `phi = 0` cells use `epsilon = 0` without calibration. The record states that independence between blocks is an approximation and that no Hoeffding coverage guarantee is claimed for the block means.

Evaluation. Seven cells x 1000 replicates x six methods, seeds 440001-441000; the population target of every method is the N interval of the same law at the true `f` and the same `theta`. Only the six primary cells enter the hypotheses.

- H8a (existence of the problem): for the naive method, Monte Carlo Clopper-Pearson intervals of `contains_population` at error 0.05/6 per cell; an upper limit below 0.95 in any cell is a refutation signal for independent treatment, otherwise "not detected". Prior prediction: a failure in the `s = 0.8` cells.
- H8b (recovery by a treatment): for thinning to every tenth window and for blocks of 200, over all 12 cell-method combinations at error 0.05/12; any upper limit below 0.95 is a refutation signal; otherwise all lower limits at least 0.93 excludes a large loss; otherwise inconclusive. Decisions only when the seed grid is complete.
- Width (descriptive): the mean width of each treatment minus the independent baseline, with seed-paired 10,000-resample bootstrap 95% intervals per cell.
- Hand-off rule for the occupancy record: among the treatments passing H8b, the one with the smaller equal-weight mean of fallback-inclusive mean widths over the six primary cells; ties within 1e-8 go to thinning to every tenth window; if neither passes, the occupancy analysis proceeds as application-only without a coverage claim. This rule is not a certification of independence or coverage on real data.

## 8. Recorded occupancy series (article Section 8.4)

Design record and implementation contract of 2026-10-14 (data not yet obtained), and an amendment of 2026-10-14 written after the data had been obtained and their time structure inspected, before any interval, width or coverage was computed. Configurations: `extensions/configs/addendum_e7_e9.json`, key `e7`; `extensions/configs/room_occupancy_manifest.json`.

Data selection rule (fixed before any data were opened; decided from metadata, schema and license only, not from detector performance or interval results). The first candidate, in the stated order, meeting all four conditions: (1) a public license permitting redistribution or reproduction; (2) a binary true state per time point recorded by a means other than the detector (human annotation, camera); (3) a detector present in the raw data or fixable from a separate training period without further training; (4) two or more units (days, rooms, persons). Candidate order: (a) UCI Room Occupancy Estimation (occupancy count and passive-infrared motion sensors); (b) UCI Occupancy Detection; (c) if neither qualifies, report before searching further.

Definitions fixed in the manifest after obtaining the data and before viewing results. Time step `Delta` = the raw resolution (secondary: `5 Delta`); `X_t` = occupied (count above 0); `Y_t` = detector output (OR of the passive-infrared sensor columns); window `(t-1, t, t+1)` with exactly equal spacing, within one unit and day; the population is restricted to one time-of-day stratum chosen from time information only, primary stratum local weekdays 09:00 to 17:00 with all three points inside; the stratum is not changed after results; `5 Delta` windows are a separate descriptive sensitivity analysis without interpolation. Duplicate time stamps are rejected; rows with missing or invalid values are removed and spacing rechecked; the time zone is required.

Evaluation (finite population). The whole stratum record is the auditable population; the record-level `theta` is the mean first-pair spin product over all eligible windows; reference intervals under M, N and F are computed from the empirical window law as it is, without stationarization, with the stationarity residual and the exact program status recorded (an empty or failed reference leaves `contains_population` undefined and is not confused with containment of `theta`). The dependence treatment is the one selected by the hand-off rule of Section 7 (thinning to every tenth center per day; blocks of 200 per day; or, for an explicit application-only input, the naive Clopper-Pearson procedure, recorded as descriptive). Passing the hand-off is not promoted to independent-episode eligibility on real data. Outputs are labeled finite-population descriptive only: the record's `f`, the conditional false-negative rate `P(Y = 0 | X = 1)`, `theta`, reference residual and status, raw empty, failure and fallback counts, mean widths and ratios, point-target containment, and interval containment only when the reference is defined. The case criteria of the design record, `f <= 0.01` and a relative width reduction of N relative to M of at least 10% at `B = 200`, are marked as descriptive case criteria; a blocked gate, insufficient sample or undefined width ratio gives `not_evaluable`. No real-world coverage statement is produced.

Interpretation fixed in the design record: if the record's `f` is small (at most 0.01) and N is meaningfully narrower than M (relative reduction at least 10% at `B = 200`), the record is presented as an application case in which the neighbor audit carries meaningful information; if `f` is large, the record is presented as a case in which the one-sided assumption does not fit, together with the bounded-false-positive results; if the intervals are close to [-1, 1] or the gate blocks, this is reported as a limitation; no other data set is substituted to obtain a better result.

## 9. SPHERE house recordings (article Section 8.4, Supplementary Table S6)

Design record of 2026-10-22, revised after an independent review of the data sources and fixed before any cross-tabulation of `X` and `Y` was computed. Before fixing it, only the README, the data paper, file listings, `meta.json` and file headers had been read. Implementation: `extensions/scripts/sphere_pir.py`; data: `data/raw/sphere_house/` (see `DATA_SOURCE.md` there).

Data choice. A public record with passive-infrared sensors and room labels derived independently of the sensors was required. The SPHERE house recordings (Tonkin et al. 2023, CC BY 4.0) have room locations annotated from a head-mounted camera. Before the run, a second candidate, the CASAS smart-home collection, was rejected: its activity labels, including leaving and entering home, were produced by annotators working from the same sensor stream, so one-sided error would partly hold by construction. The SPHERE analysis is descriptive. It checks the assumptions (one-sided error, the false-positive mass, whether the record's target lies inside the intervals) on a scripted record of ten young adults in one house and does not validate an application.

Variables. Only the `raw` view of the dataset is used (19 sessions; the files are stored under `data/raw/sphere_house/raw/NNN/`).
- Room: the living room, fixed from Table 3 of the data paper (most time and entries). The kitchen is a secondary room.
- Grid: cells `[c g, (c+1) g)` from the session start, with `g = 10 s`.
- `X_c = 1` if the mean over the session's annotators of the share of the cell labeled "living" exceeds 0.5. A cell is unlabeled if the mean labeled share (any room) is at most 0.5.
- `Y_c = 1` if a living-room activation interval in `pir_raw.csv` overlaps the cell.
- Windows: `(c-k, c, c+k)` within one session, with `k = 1`. In the primary analysis they are non-overlapping (centers `k, 3k+1, ...`). Windows touching an unlabeled cell are dropped.
- Record target: `theta_hat = 1 - 2 #(X_{c-k} != X_c) / n`.

Procedure. As in Sections 8.1 and 8.4.
- Observation moments come from all windows.
- Each of 1000 draws (seed `480001 + r`) takes `B` audit and `B` calibration windows without replacement, disjoint.
- The budget is the one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper limit (0.99) on the calibration windows. The four-state program with simultaneous Clopper-Pearson moment intervals (error 0.04) is primary; the three-state program (budget 0) is a contrast.
- `B` in {50, 100, 200}, evaluated only if `n >= 2B`. An empty set is reported as the fallback `[-1, 1]` and counted; a solver failure stops the run.

Criteria, decided only in the primary analysis (living room, `g = 10 s`, `k = 1`, non-overlapping, four-state, `B = 200`):
- H1: containment of the record's target by the neighbor interval of at least 0.95, with the Wilson 95% lower limit reported. The draws are independent given the record, so this is design-based containment for this record only; containment can fail essentially only through model misfit, so H1 alone carries little information.
- H2: the neighbor mean width at least 10% below the marginal one.

Descriptive outputs, with no criterion:
- informativeness (shares of draws with upper endpoint below 1 and lower endpoint above 0);
- `f_hat`, `P(Y=1 | X=0)`, the miss rate `P(Y=0 | X=1)`;
- the share of window centers at which the sensor of the unused second bedroom is active, an external false-positive estimate;
- the exact reference intervals when feasible, with the stationarity residual. A small residual is weak evidence, because overlapping windows from one path differ in their prefix and suffix margins only by boundary terms.

Conclusions written before the run. If H1 fails, the record contradicts stationarity or the false-positive budget, and the responsible moments are reported. If H2 fails, the neighbor audit adds little on this record.

Sensitivity settings, fixed in advance and all descriptive:
1. `(g, k)` in {(5 s, 1), (5 s, 2), (10 s, 3), (20 s, 1), (30 s, 1)};
2. overlapping windows;
3. kitchen;
4. onset-only record (an activation starts in the cell);
5. activations shifted by -2, -1, +1, +2 s;
6. audit and calibration labels from annotator 0 or 1 alone, with the target unchanged;
7. the three-state program.

Per-participant `n`, `theta_hat` and `f_hat` are descriptive. Results are reported whatever their direction.

Verification. An independent re-implementation from this record's definitions reproduced the windows, the descriptive values and five finite-sample draws before the full run. The reported numbers were recomputed independently from the raw files before entering the article.
